# Sociolinguistic Invariance

### Do language models treat you differently because of how you speak?

Language models increasingly interact with people across dialects, registers,
scripts, age groups, linguistic communities, and social contexts.

Appropriate linguistic accommodation is desirable.

Unexplained changes in factuality, calibration, deference, helpfulness, or
safety behavior are not necessarily so.

**Sociolinguistic Invariance** is a replication-first research framework for
testing whether language-model behavior remains substantively reliable when
semantic content is held constant but socially meaningful linguistic form
changes.

Greek is the first testbed.

> **Core principle: Style may adapt; epistemic standards should not silently drift.**

---

## Research question

When the underlying meaning and task remain constant, does a language model
make substantively different decisions depending on how the user sounds
socially?

The project distinguishes between:

- legitimate linguistic accommodation;
- sociolinguistically conditioned behavioral variation;
- potentially undesirable epistemic or safety drift.

---

## Initial variation conditions

The first Greek benchmark will investigate controlled transformations across:

- Standard Modern Greek
- formal/institutional Greek
- informal/colloquial Greek
- Greeklish

Dialectal and diaspora varieties will be introduced only after appropriate
speaker/expert validation.

---

## Initial behavioral dimensions

The first experiments will focus on:

- epistemic invariance;
- false-premise correction;
- deference;
- uncertainty calibration;
- benign-refusal disparity;
- accommodation/content separation.

---

## Research philosophy

This project follows a replication-first methodology.

Interesting exploratory effects are not treated as findings until they survive
evaluation on independently constructed, held-out semantic families.

Mechanistic interpretation is conditional on robust behavioral replication.

Null results and failed replications are results.

---

## Relationship to previous work

This project develops ideas explored in:

- [`indexical-circuits`](https://github.com/Rinetta1981/indexical-circuits)
- [`register-feature-family`](https://github.com/Rinetta1981/register-feature-family)

Together, the projects investigate how socially meaningful linguistic form is
represented by language models, when it affects model behavior, and which
effects survive independent replication.

---

## Museum application

The project will also provide the research engine for **Future Greek Lab —
Human vs Machine**, a prototype interactive exhibit for the proposed Museum of
the Greek Language.

The museum-facing experience will allow visitors to explore a central question:

> The speaker changed. The meaning did not. Should the AI's judgment have changed?
